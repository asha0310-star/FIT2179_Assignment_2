A Crop That Conquered the World

In sixty years, palm oil grew from a small West African export into the world's most-traded vegetable oil. Malaysia and Indonesia produced almost all of that growth.

Source: Our World in Data, FAOSTAT.

From a Few Estates to Millions of Hectares

In 1961 fewer than 55,000 hectares of Malaysia were planted with oil palm. By 2024 the figure was 5.6 million — an area roughly the size of Croatia.

The Forest Pays the Bill

Between 2001 and 2024 Malaysia lost roughly 9.5 million hectares of tree cover — close to a third of the country's land area. The losses are concentrated in the same states where palm oil expanded the fastest.

Where the Oil Goes

In 2024 Malaysia exported 16.9 million tonnes of palm oil worth RM73 billion. Just seven markets bought more than half of it.

What Comes Next

The country has begun replanting older estates and tightening sustainability certification (MSPO became mandatory in 2020). Whether forest cover can recover depends on whether we treat palm oil as land that was something else — or as land that still is.
